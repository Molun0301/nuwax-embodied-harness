It supplements [the dispatch contract diagnosis](navigation-dispatch-drift-contract-20261013.md) and [retained exact evidence](navigation-replan-trigger-audit-20261013.json); it does not reopen the separately qualified local-recovery repair.

## Classification

- **Demonstrated: sampled-state authorization does not establish the required receipt-state stopping invariant.** Attribution-v1 and v2 admitted commands whose complete receipt-state tails fail. For v1, the exact captured guard pose passes and receipt pose fails with both production geometry callbacks and the independent geometry checks. Both prior and selected commands are pure rotation at 0.8 rad/s. This establishes a geometric consequence of changing the starting state, rather than a changed requested speed. It is not a demonstrated broken received-pose/version fence: the latest received sample can remain current while the plant moves.
- **Demonstrated: the executed ideal plant and the stopping oracle have different dynamics.** The plant immediately installs the requested twist, integrates it until its deadline, then sets velocity to ZERO without a braking tail; fencing also zeros it immediately. The oracle additionally checks finite-rate velocity transitions, reaction delay and complete braking. Therefore legal executed edges do not discharge the mandatory stopping invariant. Conversely, a failed hypothetical braking tail is not a recorded physical collision. This model difference exists, but does not explain away the guard-pass/receipt-fail comparison, which uses the same stopping generator on both states.
- **Still unresolved: which production time contract a correction must preserve.** The missing contract is the relationship between sample acquisition, command activation/hold and the origin of the calibrated observation-to-braking delay. The evidence is not ambiguous about the existing receipt oracle failing; it is insufficient to select a safe physical deployment correction or to claim that callback, acceleration-model, or transport defects caused this particular failure.

## The hold-cap distinction does not erase these failures

The fixture sets the effective deadline to the earliest of receipt + 50 ms, the original worker deadline, and the source dispatch deadline. Its receipt oracle always starts a full 50 ms hold plus the unchanged configured reaction/braking checks at receipt. Those are distinct definitions and should both be recorded.

However, the worker horizon is **250 ms**, not 50 ms ([runtime constants, lines 90–103](../../services/nuwax-workspace/crates/nuwax-nav-runtime/src/lib.rs#L90)). Their source ages were about 189 ms and 192 ms against a 1-second source bound; their guard traces contain no tighter observation bound. The full retained logs show a new `step` just after the preceding APPLIED, then selected-state rechecks before the next receipt roughly one control tick later. This supports ample remaining worker/source time for receipt + 50 ms, rather than a shortened hold explaining the rejection. It is not an exact effective-deadline record.

**Trace caveat:** the approximately 26.712 ms and 1.064 ms final-computation-to-receipt intervals are not original worker-lease ages. [`planner_worker.rs:191–202`](../../services/nuwax-workspace/crates/nuwax-nav-runtime/src/planner_worker.rs#L191) preserves monotonic `started` across rechecks while sampling `compute_start_unix_ns` anew for each replay. Do not treat those two fields as a simultaneous clock pair. The intervals are retained-timestamp subtraction, not new measurements.

The plant later receives a fence before that 50 ms cap in both retained runs. A later early stop cannot retroactively authorize an unsafe receipt-state tail. Receipt history does not store the exact effective monotonic deadline, so a future paired replay should make that value explicit rather than infer it from different clock reads.

## What is already answered, and what is not

1. The v1 reconstructed differential already answers the same-geometry callback/sample-step question: both callback families agree on guard acceptance, receipt rejection and occupied cell `[17,29,9]`. It does not reconstruct historical source health. The v2 approximately 1.133 ms sample-to-receipt case has a captured failed receipt, but no retained exact two-state reconstruction.
2. Neither failed v1/v2 log records the failing callback's elapsed time or stopping branch. The later v1 reconstruction records a body witness/quaternion, not branch/time. Therefore exact attribution to hold, reaction interval or a particular braking segment is **not logged**. Do not turn the witness into a claim of actual collision at a measured time.
3. Current [`independent_tail` / `validate_history`, lines 403–465](../../services/nuwax-workspace/crates/nuwax-nav-runtime/src/scheduler_detour_cancellation_tests.rs#L403), already report callback kind, elapsed time, geometry witness, captured/receipt comparisons, exact sample-step comparisons and production callback replay when a failure occurs. The later passing v3 runs did not produce those failure details. Both production and this independent geometry oracle call the same stopping-trajectory generator; their agreement does not independently validate the dynamics model.
4. The API calls `reaction_time_sec` observation-to-braking-effect delay ([definition, line 75](../../services/nuwax-workspace/crates/nuwax-nav-types-messages/src/navigation_capabilities.rs#L75)). The generator places its longest reaction delay after the candidate control period. Restarting the complete check at receipt moves that complete future tail forward. Whether an external calibration budgets elapsed sample-to-activation motion inside this delay remains unspecified; subtracting elapsed time now would silently change the required oracle.

## Smallest useful next diagnostic

Use the existing exact reconstruction and failure helpers, without rerunning the wall-clock recovery workload or adding production state:

1. Reconstruct both retained guard/receipt pairs on identical fixed geometry and unchanged limits. Preserve the original full-tail receipt verdict as the mandatory result. Record trajectory branch, callback kind, elapsed time, velocity and first rejected swept edge/witness. Existing elapsed callbacks cover most of this; branch identity is the remaining label needed to interpret time resets.
2. Beside that result, name and evaluate two separate timelines: **ideal execution** (captured command/expiry/fence transitions, with its existing instantaneous zero), and **required stopping envelope** (sample-to-activation prefix, candidate hold, unchanged reaction delay and full braking). Never use ideal-execution success to override the required envelope. Keep control step 50 ms; changing it to a residual hold would also change reachability and discrete braking.
3. Supply one correlated event ledger: pose acquisition state/time; applied twist changes and expiries through activation; receipt/activation time; original selection start, final replay start, effective hold deadline and its binding cause; stop request and assumed braking onset. Exact activation is available in a deterministic ideal-model replay. Historical Unix and monotonic reads are separate; do not manufacture an exact historical timeline from them. For these pure-turn cases, verify the constant 0.8 rad/s prefix from the retained history; do not assume latest twist is a general history substitute.

The diagnostic is complete when it distinguishes the unmodified receipt invariant from the ideal executed path, identifies the rejected branch/segment, and reproduces both exact input pairs. It need not prove arbitrary delays. A production fix would additionally require a stated activation uncertainty/hold contract and clock relationship; the Python guard's post-SDK APPLIED timestamp and `Move` call do not provide those ([core.py:577–600](../../services/navigation/motion_safety_guard/core.py#L577), [go2_actuator.py:497–502](../../services/navigation/motion_safety_guard/go2_actuator.py#L497)).

This keeps the next step small and test-only. The existing multi-period checker explicitly excludes arbitrary activation-delay proof ([control API, lines 213–217](../../services/nuwax-workspace/crates/nuwax-nav-control/src/lib.rs#L213)). Neither extra discrete cutoffs nor wake-on-completion alone supplies that proof, and faster completion must not change the 20 Hz reachability semantics.
